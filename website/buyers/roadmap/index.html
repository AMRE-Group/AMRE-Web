The Roadmap · How the Transaction Works

The home buying process, mapped out.

Ten steps from first coffee to keys in hand. Steps 01–05 set your foundation. Steps 06–10 carry you from signed contract through closing.

Preparing for purchase · Steps 01–05
01

Buyer Consultation

First we sit down over coffee, get to know each other, and figure out how we can best help you find the right home. Goals, timeline, budget, neighborhoods, and what you actually want your Saturday mornings to look like. And don't worry — Michael always knows the perfect coffee spot.

02

Buyer Representation Agreement

We create a mutual business agreement to ensure transparency. It outlines obligations, term length, compensation, termination, exclusivity, representation, and property criteria. In California this has to be signed before we can show or send you properties — so we walk through it together first. See what's actually in it →

03

Get Pre-Approved

If you're financing, pre-approval is the important first step. We can connect you with trusted lenders and loan brokers, or you're welcome to use your own bank. A pre-approval strengthens your offer and gives you real numbers for your down payment, closing costs, and estimated monthly payment.

04

Find Properties That Meet Your Criteria

Using Compass Collections, a detailed CMA, and reverse prospecting to connect with agents who may have upcoming listings, we uncover every property that meets your criteria — both on and off the market.

05

Submit & Negotiate the Offer

Once we find the right home, we build a competitive offer: price plus the terms that actually win deals, like contingency periods and closing timeline. We guide you through the decision-making so you're confident in the number before it goes out.

Contract to close · Steps 06–10
06

Sign Contract

This is the exciting part. Your offer includes the purchase price, closing date, escrow company, and earnest money deposit. Once both you and the seller agree and sign, you're under contract and one step closer to your keys.

07

Inspection, Disclosures, Reports & Repairs

We coordinate all home inspections within the agreed timeline, review disclosures and the title report, and let you approve or negotiate credits and repairs. Michael's architectural training matters here — structural questions, permit history, and renovation cost get read properly, not guessed at. Before closing we schedule a final walk-through to verify condition and completed repairs.

08

Loan & Appraisal

Your lender will request bank statements, tax records, and pay stubs. You'll have a mortgage contingency period to secure a commitment. After review, your lender issues a Clear-to-Close and sends the file to the title company. An appraisal is also completed on the property.

09

Closing

Final documents are signed, closing costs and earnest money are settled through escrow, and funds are disbursed to the seller. Once the deed is recorded with the county, ownership officially transfers to you. You're a homeowner.

10

Welcome Home

Keys and access are transferred, utilities are set up in your name, and your home warranty (if applicable) is activated. We stay a call away for anything you need as you settle in — renovation referrals, tenant placement, or just where to get your coffee.

Every transaction moves at its own pace. This map is a guide, not a guarantee. We'll walk each step with you and adjust as your search and escrow require.

Where to Live · Location, Location, Location

Finding the right spot.

Every city says location matters. In Los Angeles it's the whole game. The right house on the wrong block will underperform the right block with an imperfect house, almost every time — and here, "location" can change by the mile, the street, even which side of a single zip code you land on.

One zip code, two different worlds

Nowhere is this truer than school boundaries. Across the Westside it's common for a single zip code to split quietly into more than one school attendance area: two homes a few blocks apart, same freeway exit, same mail carrier, entirely different assigned schools. Boundaries also change over time. It's one of the most common surprises we see catch buyers off guard, and one of the first things we verify before you fall in love with a listing.

Decades on the Westside

We've built our careers here: Beverly Hills, Bel Air, Santa Monica, Venice, Pacific Palisades, Mar Vista, Culver City, Brentwood, West Adams. We know which blocks are quiet after 6pm, which streets back up at pickup time, which HOAs are a headache, and which "up-and-coming" claims actually hold up over time. That kind of knowledge never shows up in a listing photo. It shows up in the questions we ask before you ever write an offer.

Reference · Speak the Language

Key terms.

The vocabulary that shows up in a Los Angeles purchase, in plain English.

Adjustable Rate Mortgage (ARM) A mortgage with an interest rate that can change periodically based on the performance of a specific index.
Adjustment Period The time interval between changes in the interest rate or monthly payment for an ARM.
Amortization Paying off a loan through scheduled, periodic payments that include principal and interest.
Annual Percentage Rate (APR) The yearly cost of a loan expressed as a percentage, including interest and other fees.
Appraisal A lender-ordered opinion of value used to confirm the property supports the loan amount.
Assumption of Mortgage The transfer of an existing mortgage and its terms from the current owner to a buyer.
Balloon Payment A large lump-sum payment due at the end of a loan term after regular monthly payments.
Cap A limit on the interest rate or monthly payment increase in an ARM.
Cap Rate Net operating income divided by price — the return if you paid all cash.
Clear-to-Close Your lender's confirmation that underwriting is complete and the file can move to closing.
Closing Statement The document outlining the final costs and financial terms of the transaction.
Comparative Market Analysis (CMA) An analysis of recent comparable sales used to set an offer or list price.
Condominium Ownership of a unit within a multi-unit complex with shared ownership of common areas.
Contingency A condition that must be met before a contract becomes binding — such as inspection or financing approval.
Conversion Clause A provision in an ARM allowing the borrower to convert to a fixed-rate mortgage.
Cooperative Ownership of shares in a corporation that owns the property, with the right to occupy a specific unit.
Earnest Money / Escrow Deposit A deposit showing serious intent to purchase, held in escrow until closing.
Escrow A neutral third party holding funds or documents for buyer and seller until all conditions of the sale are met.
FHA Loan A mortgage insured by the Federal Housing Administration, designed for low- to moderate-income borrowers.
Fee Simple The most complete form of property ownership — unlimited duration, no conditions on use.
Finance Charge The total cost of credit, including interest and other fees, expressed as a dollar amount.
GRM Price divided by gross rent. A quick way to compare income properties.
House-Hack Living in one unit and renting the rest to cut your housing cost.
New Development A project involving newly constructed buildings or repurposing existing structures.
Private Exclusive An off-market listing shared agent-to-agent inside the Compass network before it goes public.
Title Report A record of ownership, liens, and easements affecting the property, reviewed during escrow.

Common Questions

Buying in LA, answered.

How long does it take to buy a home in Los Angeles?+

Searching takes as long as it takes — some buyers write an offer in three weeks, others tour for a year. Once an offer is accepted, a financed purchase in Los Angeles typically closes in 30 to 45 days, and a cash purchase can close in as little as two weeks. Inspections usually run in the first 7 to 17 days of escrow.

Do I have to sign a buyer representation agreement in California?+

Yes. California law requires a written buyer representation agreement before an agent shows you property. It defines scope of services, property type and geographic area, duration (typically six months), and how compensation works. Commissions are negotiable, and we review it with you line by line before you sign anything.

What is a Compass Private Exclusive?+

A Private Exclusive is an off-market home shared agent-to-agent within the Compass network before it is publicly listed. Visibility is the easy part; hearing about the right one early comes from the relationships behind those listings. More on Private Exclusives →

Can two homes in the same zip code be assigned to different schools?+

Yes, and it happens constantly across the Westside. A single zip code often splits into more than one school attendance area, so two homes a few blocks apart can be assigned to entirely different schools — and boundaries change over time. We verify the assigned school for every home we show you rather than relying on the listing or a portal.

How much do I need for a down payment?+

It depends on your loan program, not on a fixed rule. Conventional financing commonly runs from 5 to 20 percent down, FHA can be lower, and jumbo loans at higher LA price points often ask for more. Your pre-approval is where that number gets real, along with closing costs and the estimated monthly payment.

What does a buyer's agent actually do?+

Roughly fifty distinct things across a transaction — preparing a CMA, researching off-market inventory, coordinating inspections, negotiating credits, confirming loan approval and appraisal, running the final walk-through, and confirming the deed is recorded. The full list is above.
