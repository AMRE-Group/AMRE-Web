Step one — always

Figure out what you can afford.

Not the fun part? Actually, it is. Because nothing kills the joy of house hunting faster than falling in love with a place that was never in the budget. Before you tour a single home, talk to a loan broker or the lender at your bank and get pre-approved.

Pre-approval turns "someday" into a real number — what you can borrow, what your monthly payment looks like, and what sellers will take seriously. In a competitive LA market, a pre-approval letter is your backstage pass.

Good to Know

An offer is not a marriage proposal.

This is the part that surprises every first-timer — and takes all the pressure off that 48-hour decision.

Writing an offer doesn't mean you have to buy the house. It means you're under contract with contingencies — built-in exit doors while you do your homework.

Exit door 1

Inspection

A professional checks the home top to bottom. If something serious turns up — foundation, roof, plumbing — you can renegotiate or walk away.

Exit door 2

Appraisal

The lender confirms the home is worth what you're paying. If it appraises low, you have options — including walking.

Exit door 3

Loan

Your financing gets finalized. If the loan doesn't come together as planned, the contingency protects you.

Myth, Busted

You do not need 20% down.

Somewhere along the way, "20% down" became gospel. It's not. It's one option — and for many first-time buyers, not even the best one. Loan programs exist specifically to get you in the door sooner.

3.5%

FHA loans

The classic first-timer program — lower down payment, more flexible credit requirements.

3–5%

Conventional programs

Many conventional loans allow well under 20% down for qualified buyers.

0%

VA loans

Eligible veterans and service members can buy with no down payment at all.

Programs, rates and qualifications vary — your lender will walk you through exactly what fits your situation. That's why the number conversation comes first.

Think Bigger — Something We Focus On

Your first home can be your first investment.

Here's a move we love for first-timers in Los Angeles: instead of a house, buy a duplex. Live in one unit, rent out the other, and let your tenant help pay your mortgage. You're not just a homeowner — you're an investor-owner from day one.

We treat every purchase as an investment, and with multi-family we look hard at one thing above all: cash flow. Does the rent meaningfully offset the payment? Can the property grow with you? In a market like LA, it's one of the smartest ways in.

First-Timer FAQ

The questions everyone asks.

What is the first step to buying a home?

Figure out what you can afford. Before touring anything, talk to a loan broker or the lender at your bank and get pre-approved. It turns daydreaming into a real budget — and we're happy to recommend great lenders we trust.

How long does it take to buy a first home?

Anywhere from two months to two years. There's a lot to weigh — neighborhoods, budget, timing. The funny part: you might search for months, then have about 48 hours to decide to write an offer on the right one.

Does making an offer mean I have to buy the house?

No. An accepted offer puts you under contract with contingencies — inspection, appraisal, and loan. Contingencies are your exit doors: if something serious turns up, you can renegotiate or walk away.

Do I really need 20% down?

No. That's one of the most persistent myths in real estate. Many loan programs allow far less — FHA loans start around 3.5% down, and some conventional programs start around 3–5%. Your lender will walk you through what fits.

Is buying a duplex a good first home in Los Angeles?

It can be a great one. Live in one unit, rent the other, and let the rent help cover your mortgage. We look hard at cash flow — it's one of the smartest ways to become an owner and an investor on day one in LA.
