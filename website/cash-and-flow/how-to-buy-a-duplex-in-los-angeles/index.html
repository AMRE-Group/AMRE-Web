How to Buy a Duplex in Los Angeles: The 2026 Step-by-Step Guide

Buying a duplex in Los Angeles comes down to four decisions made in order: whether you will live in it, which loan program that unlocks, whether the building is under the City of LA RSO or under AB 1482, and what the actual rent roll supports once financing is priced in. Everything else is paperwork.

The seven steps, in order

Most people buying their first two- to four-unit building in Los Angeles do these in the wrong sequence — they tour first and find out about rent control last. This is the order that keeps you from wasting a contingency period.

  1. Decide whether you will live in it

    Owner-occupancy is the single biggest fork in the road. It changes your down payment, your loan program and your rent-control obligations. Decide this before you look at a single listing, because it determines which half of the market is even available to you.

  2. Get a real pre-approval for 2–4 units

    Not every lender underwrites small multifamily well. Ask specifically how they will treat rental income, which loan limits apply to a two-, three- or four-unit property, and what reserves they want. Get it in writing before you shop.

  3. Pick the sub-markets where duplexes actually exist

    LA's two-on-a-lot stock is concentrated in specific pockets rather than spread evenly across the Westside. Narrow to those before you start touring — see the neighborhood section below.

  4. Pull the rent roll and the leases

    Current rents, not pro forma rents, are what a lender and a rational buyer underwrite. Ask for the actual leases, the deposit ledger and any written agreements with sitting tenants. A seller who cannot produce them is telling you something.

  5. Check rent-control status before you write

    Confirm whether the building falls under the City of LA Rent Stabilization Ordinance or under California's AB 1482 cap, and what that means for the tenancies in place. This is the step that most often changes the price.

  6. Underwrite the building

    Cap rate, gross rent multiplier, cash-on-cash and DSCR on real numbers — then decide what the building is worth to you, not what the list price says.

  7. Write the offer with the right contingencies

    Multi-unit diligence takes longer than a single-family escrow: rent schedules, estoppels, lender conditions. Give yourself the days up front instead of asking for extensions later.

Step 1 and 2: occupancy decides your financing

There is really one question that sets the terms of a Los Angeles duplex purchase: are you moving in?

You live in one unit

Owner-occupied

FHA allows a minimum down payment of 3.5% on owner-occupied two- to four-unit properties, and lenders will count a share of the rent from the units you do not occupy toward your qualifying income. That combination is why a duplex is often reachable when a comparable single-family home is not.

Three- and four-unit FHA purchases also have to pass a self-sufficiency test — the projected rent has to carry the payment. Ask your lender to run it on the specific building before you get attached to it.

You rent both units

Investor

No occupancy means conventional investment-property terms or a DSCR loan underwritten to the building's income rather than your W-2. Expect a materially larger down payment and pricing that reflects it.

The trade is flexibility: no occupancy requirement, no move, and the whole rent roll works for you from day one. It is also how most people buy their second building after house-hacking their first.

2026 loan limits · the number that caps your options

The Federal Housing Finance Agency set the 2026 conforming loan limit for a one-unit property at $832,750, with a ceiling of $1,249,125 in high-cost areas — Los Angeles County is a high-cost area. Limits step up for two-, three- and four-unit properties, which is a large part of why small multifamily is financeable here at prices that would be a jumbo loan on a single-family home. Confirm the current figure for the unit count you are buying in HUD's FHA mortgage limits lookup before you write.

Step 5: which rent-control regime applies

This is the question that separates a duplex that works from one that does not, and it is decided by geography and by a date, not by the listing.

Inside the City of Los Angeles, rental units in buildings with a certificate of occupancy issued before October 1, 1978 are generally covered by the Rent Stabilization Ordinance, which caps annual increases on sitting tenants and constrains how a tenancy can end. Newer City of LA buildings, and most of the rest of LA County, fall under California's AB 1482 cap instead. Several Westside cities layer their own ordinances on top of that.

Two practical consequences. First, a building under RSO with long-tenured tenants is usually priced off its current rents, not its market rents, and closing the gap is slow. Second, the exit strategy you assumed at the tour may not be legal. Verify the certificate of occupancy date and the registration status with the Los Angeles Housing Department during your contingency period — not from the remarks.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rent-control status and tenancy rules turn on facts specific to the property. Confirm with the Los Angeles Housing Department (LAHD) and a qualified attorney before you rely on any of it.

Step 6: underwrite it like a lender, not like a listing

Four numbers decide whether a Los Angeles duplex is a deal. None of them appear on the listing.

Cap rate

Net operating income over price. It tells you what the building yields unlevered — useful for comparing two buildings, useless for telling you what lands in your account.

GRM

Price over gross annual rent. The fastest sanity check on whether a price is anchored to income at all, and the one that exposes a pro forma quickly.

Cash-on-cash

Annual cash flow over cash invested. This is the number you actually live on, and the only one that moves when financing changes.

DSCR

Net operating income over debt service. It is how a lender decides the building can carry itself — and on an investor loan, how they decide to lend at all.

Step 3, revisited: where the duplexes are

Two-on-a-lot and small multifamily stock is not evenly distributed across the Westside. The older, denser pockets have it; the estate neighborhoods largely do not. If you are house-hacking, start where the stock and the price bands overlap with your loan.

  • West Los Angeles — Sawtelle, Palms and Rancho Park, the densest concentration of small multifamily on the Westside
  • West Adams — older stock, duplex and TIC conversions, much of it pre-1978
  • Culver City — its own rent-control ordinance on top of state law; check before you write
  • Mar Vista — bungalow courts and two-unit lots between the 405 and Venice
  • Santa Monica — deep small-multifamily stock and one of the strictest local ordinances in the county

Questions people actually ask

Can you buy a duplex in Los Angeles with 3.5% down?

Yes, if you are going to live in one of the units. FHA allows a 3.5% minimum down payment on owner-occupied two- to four-unit properties, and lets a portion of the projected rent from the other unit count toward qualifying income. If you will not occupy the property, expect a conventional or DSCR investor loan with a much larger down payment.

What is the difference between RSO and AB 1482 for an LA duplex?

RSO is the City of Los Angeles Rent Stabilization Ordinance and generally applies to rental units in a building with a certificate of occupancy issued before October 1, 1978. Newer City of LA buildings, and most of the rest of LA County, fall under California’s statewide AB 1482 cap instead. Which one applies changes what you can do with rents and with tenancies, so confirm status before you write an offer.

How do I find out if a duplex is under LA rent control?

Check the certificate of occupancy date and the property’s registration status with the Los Angeles Housing Department. Do not rely on the listing remarks. Confirm it in writing during your contingency period, and have a real estate attorney review anything you are unsure about.

Does the tenant in the other unit help me qualify for the loan?

Usually yes. Lenders count a share of documented or appraiser-estimated rent from the units you will not occupy as qualifying income. The exact share and the documentation required vary by loan program, so get the calculation from your lender in writing before you shop.

What should a duplex cash-flow in Los Angeles?

There is no single number. What matters is the specific building’s rent roll against its real expenses and today’s financing. That is why AMRE publishes full underwrites of actual LA duplexes rather than averages — you can see cap rate, gross rent multiplier, cash-on-cash and DSCR on real listings and compare them to whatever you are looking at.

Is house hacking a duplex better than buying a condo in LA?

They solve different problems. A condo is simpler and usually cheaper to enter. A duplex you live in puts a tenant against your mortgage payment and gives you a second unit you can rent at market later, at the cost of being a landlord from day one and of a more involved loan. Run both through the same underwriting before deciding.

How long does it take to close on a duplex in Los Angeles?

Plan for longer than a single-family purchase. Multi-unit appraisals include a rent schedule, lenders ask for leases and rent rolls, and rent-control verification takes time. Build the extra diligence into your contingency timeline rather than assuming a standard escrow will absorb it.
