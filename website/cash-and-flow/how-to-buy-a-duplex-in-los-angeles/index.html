How to Buy a Duplex in Los Angeles: The 2026 Step-by-Step Guide

Live in one unit and finance as an owner-occupant: in Los Angeles County, FHA allows 3.5% down on a two-unit loan up to $1,599,375 in 2026, while conventional 5% down stops at $1,066,250. AMRE — the Compass Beverly Hills team led by Michael Abraham, licensed architect and REALTOR®, DRE# 02242095 — then verifies permits and rent rules before you offer.

A duplex is the most common way into Westside ownership we see that doesn’t require a single-family budget: you live in one unit and the other unit’s rent helps carry the mortgage. The process looks like any home purchase until two things: the financing, which changes sharply at the 2026 loan-limit lines, and the due diligence, where permits and rent rules decide what you are really buying.

Below: the financing comparison first, because it sets your price range, then the eight steps in order.

The eight steps, in order

  1. Decide: live in it, or invest

    Owner-occupancy is the biggest lever. FHA and the low-down-payment conventional options are for a primary residence, so living in one unit opens financing a pure investor can’t use.4,3 If you won’t live there, plan on investor financing such as a DSCR loan.6

  2. Get pre-approved on the right loan program

    Ask your lender to quote FHA, conventional and (if you’re investing) DSCR side by side, and to tell you whether your loan amount will be standard or high-balance. In LA County the 2026 two-unit limit is $1,599,375 for both FHA and high-balance conforming loans; the standard conforming two-unit limit is $1,066,250.1,2

  3. Back into your price from the cash you have

    Down payment rules change at the loan-limit lines, so set your search range from the tables above rather than from a monthly payment alone. Then run a specific property through the Cash & Flow calculator with real rents and today’s rate.

  4. Confirm the jurisdiction and the rent rules

    Which city the parcel sits in and when its certificate of occupancy was issued decide whether the LA RSO, a local ordinance or state AB 1482 caps the rent you inherit. Our RSO vs AB 1482 decision table walks every case.

  5. Verify it is a legal two-unit building

    Pull the certificate of occupancy and permit history (LADBS for the City of Los Angeles; the city building department elsewhere). A converted garage or an unpermitted second unit changes the loan, the appraisal, insurance and rent-control status. Michael is a licensed architect, and this is where we spend the most time.

  6. Underwrite the rents you are actually buying

    Get the rent roll, every lease and tenant estoppel certificates, and model rent growth at the cap that legally applies — not a market guess. Leases and security deposits transfer with the building; California Civil Code §1950.5 governs how deposits move to the new owner at closing.8

  7. Write the offer with real contingencies

    Keep inspection, appraisal and loan contingencies sized to a two-unit building: a general home inspection plus sewer-lateral, roof and foundation reviews where warranted, and time to read the permit file. Appraisals on two- to four-unit properties also look at rental income, so give the appraiser the leases.

  8. Close and move in

    An owner-occupant loan requires you to actually make the property your primary residence.4 Once you’re in, keep clean records of rents and expenses — they become the history a lender will look at on your next purchase.

Where an architect changes the outcome

Most duplex problems are visible in the permit file before they’re visible in the walls: a second unit that was never finaled, an addition that changed the unit count, or a building date that puts the rents under the RSO. Common Questions

Buying an LA duplex, answered.

How much down payment do I need to buy a duplex in Los Angeles?

If you will live in one unit, FHA allows 3.5% down with a 580+ FICO (loanDepot), and Fannie Mae allows 5% down on a two-unit principal residence when the loan is within the $1,066,250 standard limit (Fannie Mae Eligibility Matrix, August 5, 2026). Above that, a high-balance conventional loan is capped at 85% LTV, so 15% down. Investor DSCR loans typically require more; Kiavi publishes up to 80% LTV.

Can I use an FHA loan to buy a duplex in LA, and what is the 2026 limit?

Yes, if it will be your primary residence. FHA lends on one- to four-unit homes, and the 2026 FHA limit for a two-unit property in Los Angeles County is $1,599,375, the national high-cost ceiling (HUD, announced December 11, 2025).

Why does a conventional loan need more down on an expensive LA duplex?

Fannie Mae’s standard two-unit limit for 2026 is $1,066,250. A larger loan in LA County is a high-balance loan (up to $1,599,375), and Fannie Mae’s matrix caps high-balance two-unit purchases at 85% LTV instead of 95%.

Can I use a DSCR loan if I live in one of the units?

Generally no. DSCR loans are business-purpose investor loans; Kiavi’s published program, for example, is for non-owner-occupied properties only. If you will live there, compare FHA and conventional owner-occupant terms first.

Does rent control still apply if I live in one unit?

It depends on the city. In the City of Los Angeles, owner occupancy is not on LAHD’s list of RSO exemptions, while state AB 1482 exempts a duplex where the owner lives in one unit. See our RSO vs AB 1482 decision table for each Westside jurisdiction.

What should I check before making an offer on an LA duplex?

The certificate of occupancy and permit history (to confirm two legal units), the jurisdiction and rent rule that applies, the rent roll, leases and tenant estoppels, and which loan program your price and down payment actually fit.
