The Setup

It's a real duplex on a real corner lot at Higuera and Schaefer — entrance on Schaefer — two blocks off Downtown Culver City. 1,784 square feet of building on a 5,839-foot lot. The front unit (8595, 2BD/1BA) has a tenant paying $3,200/month. The listing projects that same unit at $4,200 — so the in-place tenant sits about $1,000/month under its own projected market rent, and the listing discloses rent control, which means you don't simply reset it. The back unit (8597, 3BD/2BA) is detached with its own yard and two-car garage — vacant now, projected at $4,400/month.

At projected rent, that's $8,600/month, or $103,200 a year — a 17.0× GRM against the $1,799,000 ask, and $899,500 a door. At just the front tenant's actual rent, the math is thinner: this is a house-hack, not a turnkey rental.

The front unit's kitchen.
The House-Hack Play

Move into the vacant 3BD/2BA back unit — its own yard, garage, and address (8597) — and let the front tenant's $3,200/month hit your mortgage from day one. The listing flags the detached two-car garage as a possible third unit, guest house, or ADU — the sheet itself records no existing ADU, so treat it as upside to verify, not income.

The front unit's living room — original hardwood floors, updated in place.
Living There

Downtown Culver City — The Platform, the Arts District, the restaurant row on Culver and Washington — is a short walk away. Sony, Amazon, and Apple all have offices within a couple miles, a big reason rents here have held up. It's a quieter, tree-lined pocket minutes from all of it.

The Setup

Two nearly identical 1,633-sqft homes, each 3BD/2BA, stacked into one 1929 Spanish building — as the listing puts it, “two real homes, one remarkable property.” Both keep the original arches, formal dining rooms, and tiled fireplaces, paired with new marble-and-brass baths, multi-zone HVAC, and a private balcony upstairs. Parking: six spaces, four in a detached garage.

It's delivered 100% vacant and sold as-is — no lease to inherit, but also no income to point to yet. Every rent dollar here is a projection.

Seen through the original arches — one of the two matching units.
The Move-In-Ready Play

Both vacant means a clean live-in-one, rent-the-other setup, or two households sharing one roof — no tenant to wait out. The flip side: you're underwriting on comps, not a check that's already clearing.

The original tiled fireplace and arched openings, restored — not replaced.
Living There

Leimert Park Village is one of LA's most distinct neighborhood cores — independent jazz clubs and Black-owned restaurants and galleries that have anchored the area for decades, a stone's throw from the highly anticipated new Baldwin Hills Crenshaw Plaza. Metro's K Line runs through, connecting toward Inglewood and the Westside. It's one of the last pockets in LA where a restored 1929 property still lists under $450/sqft.

The Read

Same price bracket, same duplex structure, two different location bets. Higuera is the surer, more established play — walkable, job-anchored, tenant already in place. 43rd Pl is still writing its next chapter — a blank canvas in a pocket of LA that keeps pulling in more investment. Neither is “right” — they're built for different buyers.

Pick Higuera If…

You want a proven, walkable community.

You'd rather move into the vacant, private yard-and-garage unit and let an existing tenant's rent hit the mortgage immediately — and you want a thriving, walkable community with established, higher-income jobs and great schools.

Pick 43rd Pl If…

You want to be early.

You're a trend-starter, excited this neighborhood keeps seeing huge investment and a cool entrepreneurial upswing — wrapped in a bow if you love classic California Spanish architecture that can't be replaced.
