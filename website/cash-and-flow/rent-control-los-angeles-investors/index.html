Rent Control for Los Angeles Investors: RSO vs. AB 1482, Explained (2026)

Three laws can govern one Los Angeles rental. Check them in order: the City's Rent Stabilization Ordinance covers units first built on or before October 1, 1978; the City's Just Cause Ordinance covers most other City rentals; statewide AB 1482 is the backstop. The strictest one that applies is the one you underwrite to.

Last updated: September 22, 2026

Start with the address, not the listing

Almost every rent-control mistake we see in a Los Angeles duplex deal starts the same way: a buyer reads “not rent controlled” in the remarks and prices the deal at market rent. Rent control here is not one rule. It is a stack of three, and which layer bites depends on where the parcel sits and when the units were built — not on what the listing says. Here is the order to work through before you write an offer.

Layer one: the City of LA Rent Stabilization Ordinance

The RSO is the strictest layer and the one that decides most Westside and South LA duplex deals. The Los Angeles Housing Department applies it to rental units in the City first built on or before October 1, 1978, and the covered list is broader than most buyers expect: apartments, condominiums, townhomes, duplexes, two or more single-family dwelling units on the same parcel, ADUs and JADUs, hotel or motel rooms occupied by the same tenant for 30 or more consecutive days, and residential units attached to commercial buildings.

Read that list again with a house-hack in mind. A 1926 bungalow with a second cottage behind it is two single-family units on one parcel — covered. A 1950s duplex with a garage conversion permitted as an ADU last year: the ADU sits on a pre-1978 parcel, and LAHD names ADUs on its covered list. Neither of those reads as “an apartment building” to a first-time investor, and both are the kind of property AMRE's Cash & Flow desk underwrites every week.

What RSO coverage actually does to you

Once every 12 monthsRent on a covered unit may be increased once every 12 months, by the allowable percentage LAHD publishes.
90% of CPI, 1–4%Effective February 2, 2026 the allowable-increase formula is 90% of CPI within a 1% to 4% band. The percentage for the period beginning July 1, 2025 was 3%.
No utility add-onBeginning February 2, 2026 a landlord can no longer include an additional percentage increase for utilities.
Registration and surchargesCovered units are registered with LAHD. Monthly pass-through surcharges of $1.61 (RSO) and $2.83 (SCEP) require proper written notice.

The allowable percentage moves, so treat any number in a listing, a pro forma or an older blog post as stale until you have pulled the current figure from LAHD's own bulletin. That includes this page: the figures above are the published rules as of the date stamped at the top, not a quote for your building.

Layer two: the Just Cause Ordinance

A City of Los Angeles property that is too new for the RSO is not unregulated. The Just Cause Ordinance reaches most residential rentals the RSO does not, including single-family homes and condominiums. Protection attaches once the tenant has been in place six months or the initial lease has expired, whichever comes first.

The JCO is an eviction-side rule rather than a rent cap, and that distinction is where investors get surprised. You may have room on the rent and no room on the exit. Relocation obligations attach to no-fault terminations, and for a single-family dwelling under the JCO where the owner holds no more than four units, that assistance is one month's rent at the time the notice was served.

Layer three: AB 1482, the statewide backstop

California's Tenant Protection Act — AB 1482 — is what applies when no local ordinance does. It caps annual increases at 5% plus regional CPI, or 10%, whichever is lower, and it adds just-cause protection once a tenant has been in place twelve months. For no-fault terminations it requires relocation assistance of one month's rent, or a waiver of the final month's rent, within 15 days of the notice. The statute is currently set to expire January 1, 2030.

Two features of AB 1482 matter more to small investors than the cap itself:

  • The 15-year exemption rolls. Housing built within the last fifteen years is exempt — and that window moves forward every year, so a building that was exempt when the seller bought it may not be exempt when you do.
  • The owner-occupied duplex exemption is real, and it is state law only. A duplex where the owner occupies one of the two units as a principal residence is exempt from AB 1482. It is the single most useful exemption for a house-hacker, and also the most misread.

The trap worth naming

“Owner-occupied duplexes are exempt” is true of AB 1482. It does not, by itself, lift the City of Los Angeles RSO off a pre-October-1978 building. If you are buying an older LA duplex to live in one side of, you need both questions answered separately: does the RSO cover this parcel, and does a state exemption apply. Getting a yes on the second does not answer the first.

Other cities, other rules

Cross a city line on the Westside and the ordinance changes with it. Santa Monica, West Hollywood and Culver City each run their own rent-control and just-cause regimes with their own coverage dates, allowable increases, registration requirements and relocation schedules. Unincorporated Los Angeles County is a separate regime again.

Practically, that means a Westside investor comparing two similar duplexes is often comparing two different legal products. One of the reasons our Santa Monica, Culver City and West Adams guides read differently from one another is that the rules under the price are different.

Ellis Act, in one paragraph

The Ellis Act is the state law that lets an owner withdraw a rental property from the market entirely. It is all units, not one unit; it carries filing requirements, notice periods, relocation payments and multi-year restrictions on putting the property back into rental use. It is a strategy that gets planned with counsel well before closing, never a fallback you reach for after a tenant says no to a buyout.

What this does to the underwrite

Rent control converts in-place rent from a starting point into a ceiling. On a covered building you grow the actual rent roll at the allowable increase, not at market, and you treat the gap between in-place and market rent as value you capture only on a lawful vacancy you cannot schedule. That is why every breakdown we publish leads with the numbers at today's rent.

The four diligence items

1. Confirm coverage in writing. Check the address on ZIMAS and confirm RSO status with LAHD at 866-557-7368. A seller's characterization is not a source.
2. Get tenant estoppels. Actual rent, actual deposit, actual start date, actual side agreements. The rent roll is the seller's story; the estoppel is the tenant's.
3. Ask for the registration history. On a covered building, gaps and rent levels on file with LAHD tell you what the lawful rent is — which is not always what is being collected.
4. Price the exit, not just the entry. Relocation exposure, notice periods and the cost of an owner move-in belong in the model before you remove contingencies.

Questions investors actually ask

Is my Los Angeles duplex under rent control?

If the property sits inside the City of Los Angeles and the units were first built on or before October 1, 1978, it is almost certainly covered by the Rent Stabilization Ordinance. LAHD lists apartments, condominiums, townhomes, duplexes, two or more single-family units on one parcel, ADUs and JADUs among covered property types. Check the address on ZIMAS before you write an offer, and confirm with LAHD.

What is the difference between the LA RSO and AB 1482?

The RSO is a City of Los Angeles ordinance that caps increases on pre-October 1978 units and is administered by LAHD. AB 1482, the statewide Tenant Protection Act, is the backstop for units no local ordinance covers. RSO is stricter and takes precedence where it applies, so you check local first and state second.

How much can I raise the rent on an RSO unit?

LAHD sets the allowable annual increase and publishes it; a unit may be increased once every 12 months. The allowable percentage was 3% for the period beginning July 1, 2025, and effective February 2, 2026 the formula became 90% of CPI within a 1% to 4% band. Landlords may also no longer add a percentage for utilities as of that date. Pull the current figure from LAHD rather than from any number quoted in a listing.

Does the owner-occupied duplex exemption apply to me?

Under AB 1482, a duplex is exempt from the state rent cap and just-cause rules when the owner occupies one of the two units as a principal residence. That exemption is a state-law exemption. It does not by itself lift the City of Los Angeles RSO from a pre-1978 building, which is why house-hackers buying older LA duplexes need both questions answered, not one.

Do I have to pay relocation assistance if I want to move into a unit I just bought?

Owner move-in is a no-fault reason, and no-fault terminations carry relocation obligations. Amounts and eligibility differ by which ordinance governs the unit, the length of tenancy and whether the household is a qualified tenant, and LAHD publishes the schedules. Price relocation into the deal before you assume a unit will be delivered vacant.

Can I take a rent-controlled building off the rental market?

The Ellis Act is the state law that lets an owner withdraw an entire rental property from the market. It is an all-units, one-way process with filing requirements, notice periods, relocation payments and multi-year restrictions on re-renting the property. It is not a way to clear a single unit, and it should be planned with counsel long before closing.

How does rent control change the way I underwrite an LA duplex?

It turns in-place rent into a ceiling rather than a starting point. On an RSO building you underwrite the actual rent roll growing at the allowable increase, not at market, and you treat any gap between in-place and market rent as value you only capture on a lawful vacancy. That is why our breakdowns show the numbers at today's rent first.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rent-control rules change, and coverage turns on facts specific to your parcel and your tenancy. Confirm RSO status and current allowable increases with the Los Angeles Housing Department (housing.lacity.gov, 866-557-7368) or your attorney before you rely on any of it in a transaction.
