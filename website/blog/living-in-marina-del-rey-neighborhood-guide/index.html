Living in Marina del Rey: Is It Worth Moving To?

The largest man-made marina in the country, a bike path to Venice and Santa Monica, and the most accessible waterfront pricing on the Westside — an honest look at Marina del Rey in 2026 for buyers moving here from out of state.

Michael Abraham
Principal Agent, Architect · Sep 28, 2026 · 6 min read

If you're weighing living in Marina del Rey — especially moving here from out of state — the short answer is that it's the most resort-style, lowest-maintenance address on the Westside: the largest man-made small-craft harbor in the country, waterfront restaurants, and a bike path straight to Venice and Santa Monica.

Is Marina del Rey a good place to live?

For a specific kind of buyer, yes. Marina del Rey is built almost entirely around its harbor — condo and townhome towers line the water, with restaurants, a bike path to Venice and Santa Monica, and boat slips instead of backyards. It appeals to boaters, young professionals working in nearby Silicon Beach and Playa Vista, and downsizers who want a low-maintenance, resort-style lifestyle without owning a house.

Is it expensive?

Marina del Rey is the most accessible Westside waterfront address by price. The median sale price — driven mostly by condos — is about $847,000 (roughly $611 per square foot), up about 19% year-over-year. Homes are taking about 115 days to sell on average, and Redfin scores the market a low 18 out of 100 on competitiveness — meaningfully more room to negotiate than Venice or Santa Monica next door.

Who moves here.

Marina del Rey draws boaters and sailors who want a slip out their back door, young professionals commuting to Silicon Beach and Playa Vista tech offices, and out-of-state buyers who want waterfront living without Venice or Santa Monica pricing. It's also a common landing spot for people relocating to LA who want to rent or buy quickly in a walkable, amenity-rich building while they get oriented to the city.

Lifestyle & housing stock.

Life here centers on the harbor: waterfront dining, paddleboarding and sailing, and the bike path connecting straight to Venice Beach and Santa Monica. Unlike Venice or Brentwood, the housing stock is almost entirely condo and townhome towers built from the 1970s through the 1990s, so buyers are evaluating HOAs, building quality, and slip availability rather than lot size or architectural pedigree.

If you're moving from out of state.

The most common questions from relocating buyers: is it walkable (yes, within the marina, though a car helps for everything else), is it a good investment (prices are up sharply this year with slower days-on-market, which typically signals room to negotiate before it tightens), and what's the catch (HOA dues and building age vary widely — that's where we spend the most diligence time with clients).

Living here at a glance

  • Currently a buyer-friendlier market than Venice or Santa Monica — 115 days on market, low competition score.
  • Housing is almost entirely condo/townhome towers; HOA and building age matter more than lot size.
  • Popular landing spot for people relocating to LA who want walkable, amenity-rich living right away.

Is Marina del Rey a good place to live?

Yes, for buyers who want a resort-style, low-maintenance waterfront lifestyle — harbor views, boat slips, and a bike path to Venice and Santa Monica — rather than a traditional house and yard.

Is Marina del Rey safe?

Marina del Rey is generally considered a safe, low-crime, amenity-rich area, popular with young professionals and retirees alike, partly because it's a contained, walkable community around the harbor.

What are home prices in Marina del Rey?

The median sale price, driven mostly by condos, is about $847,000, or roughly $611 per square foot, up about 19% year-over-year as of mid-2026.

Is Marina del Rey a buyer's or seller's market in 2026?

It currently favors buyers — homes are taking about 115 days to sell on average and Redfin rates it a low 18 out of 100 on competitiveness, meaningfully calmer than neighboring Venice or Santa Monica.

What should someone moving to Marina del Rey from out of state know?

Almost all housing is condo or townhome, so review HOA dues and building age carefully, and decide early whether a boat slip matters to you — it changes which buildings make sense.
