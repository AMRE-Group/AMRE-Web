Selling after the Palisades or Eaton fire: a 2026 guide

There is no wrong answer between selling, rebuilding and waiting, but there are deadlines. This guide lays out the rules that protect you, the tax and insurance timelines that matter, and how to tell a fair offer from an opportunistic one. The short answer
  • You have time, and protection. AB 851 bars unsolicited purchase offers in the affected ZIP codes until January 1, 2027.
  • Selling doesn’t cost you your Prop 13 base. Prop 19 lets you move it to a replacement home anywhere in California within 2 years of the sale.
  • Rebuilding keeps it too. AB 245 gives January 2025 fire owners 8 years to rebuild and keep their assessed value.
  • Insurance follows you. Per the CA Department of Insurance, you can buy elsewhere and still collect full replacement cost.
  • Lots are trading. Redfin counted 107 Palisades lot sales in the three months to November 2025, at a $1.6M median.

On January 7, 2025, the Palisades fire burned 23,448 acres and destroyed 6,845 structures. The Eaton fire burned 14,021 acres and destroyed 9,419.12 Almost two years later, many owners are still deciding what to do with their land. Some are rebuilding, some are waiting for the street to fill back in, and some have decided their next home is somewhere else.

This page is for the owners weighing a sale. It covers what the law says, what the timelines are, and what we look at before advising anyone to list a lot. If you only read one line: don’t sign anything from a buyer who contacted you first until you know what your lot is worth.

Sell, rebuild or hold: the real trade-off

Rebuilding keeps your property tax base for a comparable home (AB 245 allows 8 years for these fires)3 and usually gets the most out of your insurance. The cost is time and project risk: permits, contractors and two or more years of living somewhere else. Selling the lot turns the land into cash now. Prop 19 lets you take your base-year value to a replacement home,5 and the Department of Insurance says replacement-cost benefits still apply if you buy elsewhere.8 Holding preserves your options while the neighborhood rebuilds, but you carry property tax, insurance and maintenance on a vacant lot, and some deadlines keep running.

The right answer depends on your insurance limits, your mortgage, your age and where you want to live. It rarely depends on the market alone.

Unsolicited offers: what the law says

In January 2025 the Governor made unsolicited below-market offers in listed ZIP codes unlawful by executive order. Those orders expired, and the Legislature replaced them with AB 851, signed October 10, 2025. It temporarily prohibits unsolicited real estate purchase offers in specified fire-affected ZIP codes in Los Angeles and Ventura Counties until January 1, 2027.3 As described in the bill analysis, an offer is not treated as unsolicited once you have listed or otherwise shown you want to sell. The law also requires a written attestation, signed by buyer and seller and recorded with the deed, and carries civil penalties of up to $25,000 per violation; a violation is also a misdemeanor.4

In practice: if a buyer cold-calls, texts or mails you with an offer, you do not have to engage. Report it to the Attorney General or the District Attorney if it looks like it breaks the law. And get an independent value before you consider any number.

What lots have actually sold for

Redfin counted 107 vacant-lot sales in Pacific Palisades (90272) in the three months ending November 30, 2025, against none a year earlier. The median lot price was $1.6 million in the Palisades and $1.3 million in Malibu, and investors bought 40.3% of the lots sold in the third quarter of 2025.10 Those are 2025 figures. We have not found a credible published 2026 count, so we price each lot from current closed sales on the same streets.

Lot value depends on things buyers check closely: lot size and shape, view, slope and soils, whether debris removal is complete and certified, utilities, and what the zoning and the rebuild rules allow. Federal debris removal finished on private Palisades properties in August 2025, and LA County reports Phase 2 cleanup completed in September 2025 for owners in the government program.1112 If you opted out and used a private contractor, have your clearance paperwork ready.

Taxes and insurance: the timelines

The table above has the main rules. Three are worth repeating. For federal tax, a destroyed main home is treated as sold, so the $250,000/$500,000 exclusion can apply. Gain above that, from insurance proceeds, can generally be deferred if you buy replacement property within four years after the close of the first tax year the gain is realized.7 For property tax, the Prop 19 clock is two years from your sale to buy or build the replacement, with value tiers of 100%, 105% or 110% depending on timing.5 For insurance, additional living expenses run at least 24 months from the declared emergency, which for January 2025 losses means about January 2027. Up to 12 more months are possible for delays outside your control.8 Line these dates up with a CPA before you list.

Common Questions

Selling after the fires, answered.

Can I sell my lot after the Palisades or Eaton fire?

Yes. Lots are trading: Redfin counted 107 vacant-lot sales in Pacific Palisades in the three months ending November 2025, at a $1.6 million median. Have debris-removal clearance, your survey and pre-fire permits ready, and get an independent valuation before you consider any offer.

Is it illegal to make lowball offers on fire-damaged homes?

Under AB 851, signed October 10, 2025, unsolicited real estate purchase offers are temporarily prohibited in specified fire-affected ZIP codes in Los Angeles and Ventura Counties until January 1, 2027. The law also requires a buyer-seller attestation recorded with the deed, and carries civil penalties of up to $25,000 per violation. Check the bill text or ask an attorney about a specific offer.

Will I lose my Prop 13 tax base if I sell my burned lot?

Not necessarily. Under Proposition 19, if your principal residence was more than 50% damaged by the fire, you can transfer its base-year value to a replacement home anywhere in California, provided you buy or build it within two years of selling. Selling the lot in its damaged state still qualifies. File the claim with the assessor in the county of the new home.

Can I collect my full insurance replacement cost if I buy somewhere else?

According to the California Department of Insurance, you can purchase or rebuild at another location and still receive full replacement-cost benefits, including code-upgrade and extended replacement coverage if your policy has them. Confirm the details and deadlines with your insurer.

Do I owe tax on insurance money or the sale of my destroyed home?

Possibly not. IRS Publication 547 treats a destroyed main home as sold, so the $250,000 ($500,000 joint) home-sale exclusion can apply, and remaining gain from insurance can generally be deferred if you buy replacement property within four years after the close of the first tax year the gain is realized. Your CPA should run your numbers.
