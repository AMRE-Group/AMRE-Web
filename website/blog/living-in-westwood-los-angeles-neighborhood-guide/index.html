Living in Westwood, LA: Is It Worth Moving To?

UCLA, Westwood Village, and the most central lower-cost address on the Westside — an honest look at Westwood in 2026 for buyers moving here, including for a UCLA-affiliated job or a Century City office.

Michael Abraham
Principal Agent, Architect · Sep 28, 2026 · 6 min read

If you're weighing living in Westwood — including moving here from out of state for school or work — the short answer is that it's the Westside's most accessible price point that still puts you inside the core: UCLA, Westwood Village, and a short drive to Beverly Hills, Santa Monica, and Century City.

Is Westwood a good place to live?

Yes, especially for buyers who want a central Westside location without Santa Monica or Brentwood pricing. Westwood is built around UCLA and Westwood Village, with condo towers and mid-rise buildings dominating the housing stock closer to campus, and single-family streets in the flats and hills to the north and south. Its location — central to Beverly Hills, Santa Monica, Century City, and the 405 — makes it one of the most practically located Westside neighborhoods for daily life.

Is it expensive?

Westwood is the most accessible Westside neighborhood by price among the ones we cover. The median sale price is about $1.19 million (roughly $744 per square foot), down about 2.7% year-over-year, with price per square foot down about 10.5%. Homes are taking about 67 days to sell, slightly slower than a year ago — but sales volume is actually up nearly 29%, meaning more transactions are happening even as individual homes take a bit longer.

Who moves here.

Westwood draws UCLA faculty, staff, and grad students buying instead of renting, medical professionals working at UCLA and nearby hospitals, and buyers who want a central Westside location at a lower price point than Brentwood or Santa Monica. Out-of-state relocators moving for a UCLA-affiliated job or a Century City or Beverly Hills office often land in Westwood specifically because it splits the difference on commute in every direction.

Lifestyle & housing stock.

Westwood Village anchors daily life with restaurants, theaters, and a walkable campus-town feel unlike anywhere else on the Westside. Housing closer to the Village and Wilshire Corridor is mostly condo and co-op towers; the single-family stock in Westwood's flats and hillside streets to the north offers more traditional and mid-century homes, generally at a discount to comparable homes in Brentwood or Santa Monica.

If you're moving from out of state.

The most common questions we get: is it walkable (very, near the Village and campus; more car-dependent further out), is it a good value versus Brentwood or Santa Monica (generally yes, particularly for condos), and how's the market moving (slightly slower per-listing but with meaningfully more sales happening — a sign of real, active buyer demand even as prices soften).

  • Sales volume is up nearly 29% year-over-year even as individual homes take slightly longer to sell.
  • Generally a better value than Brentwood or Santa Monica for a comparably central location.

Is Westwood a good place to live?

Yes, particularly for buyers who want a central Westside location — near UCLA, Westwood Village, Beverly Hills, and Santa Monica — without Brentwood or Santa Monica pricing.

What are home prices in Westwood?

The median sale price is about $1.19 million, or roughly $744 per square foot, as of July 2026, down about 2.7% year-over-year.

Is Westwood a buyer's or seller's market in 2026?

It's mixed — individual homes are taking slightly longer to sell (about 67 days versus 56 a year ago), but overall sales volume is up nearly 29%, suggesting real, active demand even as prices have softened.

Is Westwood a good value compared to Brentwood or Santa Monica?

Generally yes — Westwood offers a comparable central Westside location at a meaningfully lower price point, especially in its condo and co-op stock near the Village and Wilshire Corridor.

What should someone moving to Westwood know?

Housing type varies a lot by exact location — condo towers dominate near the Village and Wilshire Corridor, while single-family homes are more common in the flats and hills to the north.
