How to choose the right broker to sell your LA multifamily.

Selling an apartment building in Los Angeles isn't like selling a house. Here's what separates a great multifamily broker from one who just lists it on LoopNet.

Michael Abraham
Principal Agent, REALTOR® · October 5, 2026 · 6 min read

Selling a multifamily property in Los Angeles means selling to investors, family offices, and 1031 exchange buyers. Rent control, city inspections, and escrow friction can sink a deal that looked done. If the property sits in a trust or has several owners, the process gets harder still.

Most sellers compare brokers on commission and a valuation number. The better question is who the broker can reach before the property goes public, and how they protect your price if the first number doesn't land.

1. Access to motivated capital, not just listing sites.

Many commercial agents rely on email blasts and public sites like LoopNet. That reaches everyone, including bargain hunters.

The strongest brokers work inside high-volume offices, where agents represent high-net-worth clients, family offices, and investors on 1031 exchange deadlines. 1031 buyers are on an IRS clock. They have 45 days to identify a replacement property and 180 days to close, so they'll often pay more for a quality asset they can secure fast.

Ask your broker:

  • Who will you pitch this property to before it hits the MLS or LoopNet?
  • Which multifamily specialists, inside and outside your brokerage, will you call personally?
  • How many of your recent buyers were 1031 exchange buyers?

2. Test your top price privately before going public.

This is one of the most overlooked strategies in multifamily sales. If you list high on the open market and the property sits, days on market add up. Buyers read that as weakness and start asking for 10–15% off.

A private pre-market launch lets you test the top of your price range with a curated group of qualified buyers first. You get real feedback from motivated buyers. If the price needs to change, you can adjust it before the public listing, and your days on market stay at zero.

Ask your broker:

  • Can you run a private or office-exclusive pre-market period?
  • How long would it last, and what feedback will I get?
  • What's the plan if we need to adjust before going public?

3. Operator experience: brokers who own and manage buildings.

The best multifamily brokers have owned and managed buildings themselves. That shows up during the sale:

  • Working with your property manager: coordinating access, rent rolls, and tenant notices without friction.
  • Inspections and compliance: knowing LA's city inspection requirements and Rent Stabilization Ordinance (RSO) disclosures before they turn into escrow problems.
  • Spotting deal killers early: fixing maintenance or compliance issues before a buyer uses them to renegotiate.

4. Driving escrow to the finish line.

Most failed multifamily deals fall apart in escrow, not at the offer. Buyers retrade after inspections, stretch contingencies, or slow-walk their financing. A strong broker keeps every deadline moving, separates real issues from leverage plays, and pushes the deal to close on your terms.

5. Experience with trusts, heirs, and remote owners.

Inherited properties and trust sales come with extra layers: multiple trustees, heirs with different goals, and owners who live out of state. Look for a broker who has:

  • Handled multi-party trust sales and stays calm when there are several decision-makers.
  • Worked with remote owners, so nobody needs to visit the property before closing.
  • No ego about co-brokering: willing to bring in other brokerages if that gets you the best result.

Planning ahead for a family property? See our guide to estate planning for real estate owners.

6. A broker focused on your net, not their listing.

A broker's fiduciary duty is to you. In practice, that means aiming for the highest net price in the shortest reasonable time. It shouldn't mean holding on to the listing at any cost or chasing a quick close for a quick commission.

Key takeaways

  • Buyer access: Who sees the property before it goes public?
  • Private price testing: Can you test the top of the range without building up days on market?
  • Operator experience: Do they understand inspections, RSO, and property management?
  • Trust and remote experience: Can they run a smooth sale with several owners or out-of-state principals?
The best price on an LA apartment building is set before it ever hits LoopNet.

Frequently asked questions.

How do I choose a broker to sell a multifamily property in Los Angeles?

Look past commission and valuation. Ask who the broker can reach before the property goes public, whether they can test your price privately, whether they have owned and managed buildings themselves, and whether they have handled trust or multi-owner sales.

Why do 1031 exchange buyers matter when selling an apartment building?

1031 buyers have 45 days to identify a replacement property and 180 days to close. That deadline makes them motivated to pay for a quality asset they can secure quickly, so a broker with direct access to them can create stronger offers.

What is a private pre-market launch?

A short period before public listing where the property is shown to a curated group of qualified buyers and agents. It lets you test the top of your price range and gather feedback without accumulating public days on market.

Why do days on market hurt a multifamily sale?

When a listing sits publicly, buyers read it as weakness and push for larger discounts, often 10–15%. Testing price privately first keeps your public record clean.

Can I sell a Los Angeles property held in a trust if I live out of state?

Yes. An experienced broker can run a trust or multi-heir sale entirely remotely, coordinating trustees, property managers, and escrow so owners never need to visit the property before closing.
