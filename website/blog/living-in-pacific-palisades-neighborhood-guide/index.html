Living in Pacific Palisades: Is It Worth Moving To?

An honest, current look at a neighborhood in active recovery — over 1,000 homes rebuilding since the January 2025 wildfire, what that means for buyers, and what a move here actually looks like in 2026.

Michael Abraham
Principal Agent, Architect · Sep 28, 2026 · 6 min read

If you're weighing living in Pacific Palisades — including moving here from out of state — the honest starting point is that this is a neighborhood in active recovery. The January 2025 wildfire destroyed a large share of the housing stock, and as of late 2026 the community has crossed 1,000 homes under active rebuild. For buyers, that means Pacific Palisades today is unusually split between untouched legacy homes and new, code-current construction — which changes how you should shop it.

Is Pacific Palisades a good place to live?

For buyers who want privacy, coastal and canyon scenery, and top schools, it remains one of LA's most desirable addresses — and the active rebuild has, for some buyers, turned into an opportunity: a chance to buy new, code-current construction in a legacy neighborhood rather than gut-renovating an older home. It's not the right fit for anyone uncomfortable buying near an active rebuild zone, and every purchase here deserves a clear-eyed look at the specific street and its rebuild timeline.

What are prices doing?

The median sale price is about $3.37 million as of August 2026, down roughly 3.6% year-over-year, with price per square foot down closer to 17% — a sign the mix of what's selling has shifted significantly since the fire, including more land and rebuild-stage sales alongside intact homes. Homes are taking about 55 days to sell, faster than the prior year.

Buying a rebuild lot or new construction.

This is where an architect's eye matters most. Evaluating a Palisades purchase right now often means comparing a legacy home against a cleared lot or a home mid-rebuild — assessing permitting status, builder quality, and how a street's rebuild timeline affects both livability and resale in the next few years. We walk every Palisades client through this specifically rather than treating it like a typical resale search.

Who moves here.

Pacific Palisades has long drawn executives, entertainment professionals, and families prioritizing schools and privacy. Since the fire, it has also drawn a new segment: buyers specifically seeking new-construction opportunities in an established, amenity-rich neighborhood rather than an entirely new development elsewhere.

If you're moving from out of state.

Out-of-state buyers should plan a visit before committing — conditions vary block to block in ways that don't show up in listing photos or online data right now. We can walk a specific street's rebuild status, insurance and permitting realities, and realistic timelines before you fly out to tour.

Is Pacific Palisades a good place to live?

Yes, for buyers who prioritize coastal and canyon scenery, top schools, and privacy — though the neighborhood is in active recovery from the January 2025 wildfire, with over 1,000 homes currently rebuilding, so conditions vary significantly by street.

What are home prices in Pacific Palisades right now?

The median sale price is about $3.37 million as of August 2026, down about 3.6% year-over-year, with price per square foot down closer to 17% as the mix of what's selling shifts to include more land and rebuild-stage properties.

Is it a good time to buy new construction in Pacific Palisades?

For buyers comfortable with an active rebuild environment, yes — it's a rare chance to get new, code-current construction in an established neighborhood. We recommend an in-person visit and street-by-street review of rebuild status before committing.

Is Pacific Palisades safe to move to now?

The community has made significant rebuilding progress, with over 1,000 homes under active construction as of late 2026, but conditions and timelines still vary block by block. We walk every client through the current status of the specific street they're considering.

What should an out-of-state buyer know about Pacific Palisades?

Plan an in-person visit before committing — the rebuild status, permitting realities, and day-to-day conditions vary enough right now that listing photos and online data don't tell the full story.
