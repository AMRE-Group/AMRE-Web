Santa Monica vs Venice: where to buy in 2026

Both sit around $2 million at the median. The difference is in the rules: Santa Monica is its own city with its own rent control, schools and transfer tax; Venice is part of Los Angeles. AMRE — the Compass Beverly Hills team led by Michael Abraham, licensed architect and REALTOR®, DRE# 02242095 — works both sides of the border.

The short answer
  • Price: roughly even. Santa Monica’s Q3 2026 median was $1.99M; Venice’s closed-sale median was about $2.08M.
  • Choose Santa Monica for its own school district, city services and a lower transfer tax under $5M (0.3% vs 0.45%).
  • Choose Venice for walk streets, canals, creative-studio buildings and more single-family lots near the sand.
  • Investors: the rent rules differ. Santa Monica caps controlled units at 2.6% (max $70) this year; LA’s RSO allows 3%.
  • Over $5M: Santa Monica’s Measure GS starts at 0.6%; LA’s Measure ULA (Venice) starts at 4% from $5.4M.

Santa Monica and Venice share a beach, a bike path and a border at Rose Avenue, and buyers often tour both in the same afternoon. On price they are close. What separates them is jurisdiction: Santa Monica is an independent city, and Venice is a neighborhood of the City of Los Angeles. That one fact decides which rent rules, which transfer tax, which school district and which permit desk you deal with for as long as you own.

Here is the comparison with sources, then how the two actually feel, block by block.

Price: closer than people think

Santa Monica’s median sale price was $1.99 million in the third quarter of 2026, at about $1,200 a square foot, down 2.2% from a year earlier.1 In Venice the median closed price over the six months to November 9 was about $2.08 million,2. Different sources, different periods, so don’t read a precise gap into it. The practical point is that you are not choosing between a cheap side and an expensive side. You are choosing between two sets of rules and two very different streetscapes.

How they feel, block by block

Santa Monica is several towns in one. North of Montana is quiet single-family streets and the city’s highest prices. Ocean Park and the area around Main Street are older cottages and small apartment buildings near the beach. Sunset Park, south of Pico by the airport, is where families find yards. Mid-City and Wilshire Montana are mostly condos and townhomes close to the Expo line.

Venice works at a smaller scale. The walk streets and canals have narrow lots with houses that face footpaths and water. Abbot Kinney is studios, restaurants and architect-designed infill. Oxford Triangle and the streets east of Lincoln are quieter and closer to Marina del Rey. On Venice blocks, the house next door can be a 1920s bungalow on one side and a three-story modern on the other. To us, that variety is the appeal. To some buyers, it is the problem.

Rent rules, if you buy to rent

Santa Monica runs its own rent control under the City Charter. It covers most units in buildings built before April 10, 1979, and the 2026 general adjustment is 2.6%, capped at $70 a month, effective September 1.3 Venice falls under Los Angeles’s Rent Stabilization Ordinance for buildings built on or before October 1, 1978, with a 3% allowable increase from July 2025 through June 2027.5 Newer buildings in both places generally fall under the state AB 1482 cap, 8.7% for increases from August 2026 in the LA area.6 Single-family homes and condos are generally exempt from local rent control under state law, with exceptions. Transfer tax: the line that matters at $5M

Below $5 million, Santa Monica’s city transfer tax is 0.3% of the price and the City of Los Angeles base rate is 0.45%.47 Above it the gap gets wide. Santa Monica’s Measure GS applies 0.6% from $5 million and 5.6% from $8 million, on the whole price. Los Angeles’s Measure ULA applies 4% from $5.4 million and 5.5% from $10.9 million for closings after June 30, 2026.7 On a $6 million sale that is roughly $36,000 in Santa Monica against about $240,000 in Venice, before the base rates. ULA has been the subject of reform and repeal efforts, so check the current rate when you are pricing a sale. Our Measure ULA explainer has the background.

Building and remodeling near the sand

Both sit in the coastal zone. In parts of Venice nearest the shoreline, a project needs a coastal development permit from both the City and the California Coastal Commission. Venice has a certified Land Use Plan but no full Local Coastal Program, which adds review time.10 If you are buying to add a story, an ADU or a full rebuild, we read the permit path for that lot before you offer. It can matter more than the price.

Our short version

If schools, a quieter street and city services are the priority, start in Santa Monica, north of Montana or in Sunset Park. If you want walkability, character and a house that is not like the one next to it, start in Venice. If you are an investor, run the rent rules before the cap rate. Common Questions

Santa Monica or Venice, answered.

Is Santa Monica or Venice more expensive?

They are close. Santa Monica's median sale price was $1.99 million in Q3 2026 (PropertyShark), and Venice's closed-sale median was about $2.08 million over the six months to mid-September 2026. The sources and periods differ, so treat them as roughly even; price depends far more on the block and the property type.

Is Venice part of Los Angeles?

Yes. Venice is a neighborhood of the City of Los Angeles, so LA's rent stabilization ordinance, transfer taxes (including Measure ULA), LAUSD schools and LA permitting apply. Santa Monica is a separate city with its own council, rent control board, school district and transfer tax.

Which has lower transfer tax, Santa Monica or Venice?

Under $5 million, Santa Monica's city rate is 0.3% versus 0.45% in the City of Los Angeles. Above that, Santa Monica's Measure GS is 0.6% from $5 million and 5.6% from $8 million, while LA's Measure ULA is 4% from $5.4 million and 5.5% from $10.9 million for closings after June 30, 2026. LA County's 0.11% applies in both.

How does rent control differ between Santa Monica and Venice?

Santa Monica's Charter covers most units in buildings built before April 10, 1979, with a 2.6% increase (max $70 a month) from September 1, 2026. Venice falls under LA's RSO for buildings built on or before October 1, 1978, with a 3% allowable increase through June 2027. Newer buildings in both generally fall under the state AB 1482 cap.

Which is better for families, Santa Monica or Venice?

Many families start in Santa Monica because of the Santa Monica-Malibu Unified School District and quieter areas such as North of Montana and Sunset Park. Venice is in LAUSD, and Venice High is its public high school. School choice is personal, so visit the specific schools for any address you are considering.
