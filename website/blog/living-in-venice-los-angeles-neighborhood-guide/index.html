Living in Venice, CA: Is It Worth Moving To?

Canals, the boardwalk, and the shortest commute on the Westside to Silicon Beach — an honest look at what it costs to live in Venice in 2026, who's actually moving here, and what changes if you're coming from out of state.

Michael Abraham
Principal Agent, Architect · Sep 28, 2026 · 6 min read

If you're weighing living in Venice, CA — or moving here from out of state — the short answer is that it's one of the most distinct addresses on the Westside: canals, a world-famous boardwalk, and a fast walk or bike ride to Silicon Beach's biggest employers.

Is Venice a good place to live?

For the right buyer, yes. Venice runs from the beach and boardwalk inland past the historic canals to Abbot Kinney Boulevard, one of LA's most-walked shopping and dining strips. It sits inside the Silicon Beach tech corridor — Google, Snap, and dozens of startups are a short drive or bike ride away — which is a huge part of why remote and tech workers relocating from out of state target it specifically. The trade-off is density and street-level intensity near the boardwalk; the canals and streets east of Pacific Avenue feel calmer and more residential.

Is it expensive?

Venice is expensive by any measure. As of August 2026, the median sale price is about $2.05 million (roughly $1,050 per square foot), down about 2.4% year-over-year. Homes are taking about 58 days to sell on average — faster than the year before. Condos and smaller units bring the entry point down meaningfully versus a single-family canal house.

Who moves here.

Venice draws a specific mix: tech and creative professionals relocating for Silicon Beach jobs, second-home and investment buyers who want a walkable beach address, and longtime Angelenos trading up from renting. Out-of-state buyers moving here from cities like San Francisco, New York, or Austin often cite the same reason — nowhere else on the Westside blends beach lifestyle with this concentration of tech employers within biking distance.

Lifestyle & architecture.

Abbot Kinney anchors daily life with restaurants, boutiques, and galleries; the boardwalk and canals give Venice a character no other LA neighborhood has. Architecturally, Venice is a magnet for modern design — glass-box infill homes, post-and-beam mid-century pieces, and architect-led spec builds sit next to original 1920s bungalows, which is exactly the kind of variety we look at closely with an architect's eye before a client buys.

If you're moving from out of state.

The most common questions we get from relocating buyers: how walkable is it really (very, near the beach and Abbot Kinney; less so further inland), is parking a problem (yes, budget for it or prioritize a listing with off-street parking), and how does the market move (currently a fairly balanced, slightly buyer-friendlier market than the peak years, per the ~58-day average time on market).

Living here at a glance

  • A distinct beach-and-canal lifestyle with the shortest commute on the Westside to Silicon Beach tech employers.
  • Expensive — ~$2.05M median — but currently more balanced than the peak years.
  • Architecturally diverse: modern infill, mid-century, and 1920s bungalows side by side.
  • Parking and density near the boardwalk are the main adjustments for out-of-state buyers.

Is Venice CA a good place to live?

For many buyers, yes — Venice offers a beach and canal lifestyle with unmatched proximity to Silicon Beach employers like Google and Snap, plus the walkable Abbot Kinney corridor. It suits people who want beach living without leaving the tech job market.

Is Venice CA safe?

Like most dense, mixed-use LA neighborhoods, safety varies block by block, with the boardwalk and areas near it seeing more street-level activity than the canals and residential streets further inland. We walk clients through the specific block before they buy.

What are home prices in Venice CA?

As of August 2026, the median sale price is about $2.05 million, or roughly $1,050 per square foot, down about 2.4% from a year earlier. Condos and smaller units bring the entry point down.

What should someone moving to Venice from out of state know?

Budget for parking, expect density near the boardwalk and Abbot Kinney, and know that homes are currently selling in about 58 days on average — a more balanced market than the pandemic-era peak, which gives relocating buyers real room to evaluate.

Is Venice a buyer's or seller's market in 2026?

Fairly balanced with a slight cooling: prices are down about 2.4% year-over-year and homes are selling a week faster than last year, which points to steadier, less frantic conditions than a few years ago.
