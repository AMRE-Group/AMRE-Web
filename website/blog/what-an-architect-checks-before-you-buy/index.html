What an architect checks before you buy a Westside home

An architect checks twelve things before you offer on a Westside home: permit history, Certificate of Occupancy, open code orders, seller disclosures, foundation, soft-story status, sewer, roof, systems and jurisdiction. AMRE, the Compass Beverly Hills team led by Michael Abraham, licensed architect and REALTOR®, DRE# 02242095, runs this list before every offer.

By Michael Abraham · AMRE at Compass Updated November 12, 2026 7 min read
Updated November 12, 2026
General information, not legal, tax or lending advice. This checklist is a general guide, not an inspection or a recommendation on any property. Costs are published consumer and agency figures, not bids. Confirm permits, code orders and costs for a specific address with the city, licensed contractors and a California attorney.

Most surprises on an older Westside house are in the paper record, not the paint. We compare the permit history with what is actually built, then look at the structure and the systems. Below is the 12-item list we run before an offer, with a record to pull, a red flag and a cost range for each. A range appears only where we found a dated source; where we did not, the table says so.

How we pull the record

LADBS lists a Search Online Building Records tool and a Permit Status page, and says the Building Card (B-8) records all inspections.1 We pull both for the address, then compare the permitted plans with what we see on site: room count, additions, the garage, bathrooms and any second unit. When the record and the building disagree, that is the first conversation we have with you, before the offer.

Which city governs the address

LADBS says zoning and property information comes from ZIMAS, and that a property that does not appear there may be outside the City of Los Angeles.1 Several Westside addresses sit in other cities with their own building departments, so we confirm the jurisdiction first and then pull that department’s records.

What we left out

We found no dated, citable cost range for Certificate of Occupancy remediation, soft-story retrofit work, electrical or plumbing upgrades, or legalizing unpermitted work, so the table gives none. For each of those we ask for a written bid. We also did not quote the wording of the statutory transfer disclosure form, because the statute page we read shows it only as images.10

Common Questions

Before you buy, answered.

What does an architect check before you buy a home in Los Angeles?

An architect checks twelve things before you offer on a Westside home: permit history, Certificate of Occupancy, open code orders, seller disclosures, foundation, soft-story status, sewer, roof, systems and jurisdiction. AMRE, the Compass Beverly Hills team led by Michael Abraham, licensed architect and REALTOR®, DRE# 02242095, runs this list before every offer. Cost ranges in the table come from published sources and are not bids for any specific property. This is general information, not legal, tax or lending advice.

Where do I look up permits for a Los Angeles address?

LADBS points owners to Search Online Building Records and a Permit Status page, and tells them to keep the Certificate of Occupancy and Building Card (B-8). If the address does not appear in ZIMAS, it may be outside the City of Los Angeles and under another building department.

What happens if a home has unpermitted work?

LADBS can charge an investigation fee of double the permit fee, never less than $400, when a permit is required to bring the property into compliance, and more when substantial work was done without a permit. An open Order to Comply also carries a $356.16 inspection fee, and LADBS says unpaid fees may result in a lien. Confirm specifics for an address with LADBS and a California attorney.

Does my older Westside house need an earthquake retrofit?

The sources we read do not require one for a single-family home. CRMP says homes built before 1980 on raised foundations are especially vulnerable, and a typical brace-and-bolt retrofit by a licensed contractor may cost $3,000 to $7,000. The Earthquake Brace + Bolt grant is up to $3,000 for eligible homes.

What did AMRE leave out of this checklist?

Cost ranges for Certificate of Occupancy remediation, soft-story retrofits, electrical and plumbing work, and unpermitted-work legalization, because we could not source them. We also did not quote the line items of the statutory disclosure form, because the statute page we read shows the form only as images.
