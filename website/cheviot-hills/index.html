Cheviot Hills
real estate.

Pre-war architecture on curving hillside streets between Century City and the Rancho Park flats — where grade, boundary lines and the association question decide the deal.

Rancho Park · Overland Ave · Beverlywood Edge

Last updated: September 8, 2026

Cheviot Hills is a low-density, pre-war single-family neighborhood on the Westside of Los Angeles, between Century City and the Rancho Park flats. Spanish Colonial Revival, Tudor and Traditional houses sit on curving, hilly streets with larger and less regular lots than the flats below. Three things drive value: grade, the exact boundary line, and whether an association governs the parcel.

It is one of the quieter buys on this side of town, and it is frequently mislabelled. Listings on the Rancho Park side, the Beverlywood side and the Palms side all borrow the name, and each of those carries different rules — an HOA in one direction, denser zoning in another. The parcel decides, not the marketing.

AMRE represents buyers and sellers here and in the neighborhoods it touches. Michael trained as an architect, which matters more in Cheviot Hills than in most Westside neighborhoods: on a sloped lot, the buildable envelope, the drainage, the retaining and the construction access are the project, and reading them correctly before the offer is the whole advantage.

At a glance

Cheviot Hills — six things to know.

01

Pre-war architecture

Spanish Colonial Revival, Tudor and Traditional stock from the neighborhood's early development, much of it remodelled and some of it substantially rebuilt. Original detail, roof form and window systems are where condition surprises live.

02

Grade is the variable

These are hills, not flats. Slope shapes the buildable envelope, drainage, retaining requirements and what a renovation or addition actually costs. Two similar-looking lots can carry very different project budgets.

03

The Beverlywood edge

Adjoining Beverlywood is largely governed by a homeowners association with architectural and maintenance rules. Cheviot Hills generally is not. On a boundary street, that question comes before price.

04

Overland Avenue and schools

The neighborhood sits inside LAUSD boundaries, with Overland Avenue Elementary the school most associated with it. Assignments are by address and boundaries move — confirm with the district for the specific property.

05

Parks and golf

Cheviot Hills Recreation Center is the neighborhood green. Rancho Park Golf Course and the Hillcrest Country Club form the southern and western edges, which is part of why density stays low.

06

Century City minutes away

Offices, the mall and the medical center sit immediately north, and the Metro E Line station at Westwood and Pico is within reach of the southern edge. Short commutes without a commercial corridor running through the neighborhood.

Underwriting checklist

What we check before you remove a contingency in Cheviot Hills

Association status
Whether the parcel falls under a homeowners association — Beverlywood's in particular — and what its architectural and maintenance rules permit.
Slope and soils
Grade, drainage, retaining, geotechnical history and construction access. On a hillside lot these determine both feasibility and cost.
Permit history
What was permitted versus what is standing, especially on additions, converted garages, decks and rear structures on sloped parcels.
Boundary and jurisdiction
The exact parcel line relative to Rancho Park, Palms and Beverlywood, and the zoning that follows from it.
School assignment
The assigned LAUSD school confirmed by address rather than by neighborhood name or listing copy.
Envelope condition
Roof, windows, original detail and any earlier remodel work — the pre-war stock rewards a close look at what was done and when.

Informational only, not legal advice. Confirm association rules, zoning and permitting with the City of Los Angeles, the relevant association and your own attorney.

Pricing

How pricing actually works here.

Cheviot Hills prices on lot and light before it prices on square footage. A level or gently sloped parcel with usable rear yard, good solar orientation and straightforward construction access trades differently from a steep lot with the same house on it. Buyers who compare only the interior finish tend to overpay for the harder parcel.

The second lever is whether the house has already had its major work done. A thoughtful, permitted remodel of a pre-war house removes a large block of risk, and the market pays for that. An original house on a good lot is the better project, but it is a project.

Neighbors

Where it sits on the Westside.

South and west, down the hill, is West Los Angeles — Rancho Park, Palms and Sawtelle. Flats, smaller lots, far more attached housing, and the Westside's deepest supply of small multifamily. Buyers who want a rental unit attached to the purchase end up there rather than here.

South-east across National is Culver City, a separate incorporated city with its own school district and its own rent control. North-west is Westwood, and beyond it the rest of the Westside corridor.

Cheviot Hills FAQ

Cheviot Hills, explained.

Where exactly is Cheviot Hills?

Cheviot Hills sits on the Westside of Los Angeles between Century City to the north and the Rancho Park and Palms flats to the south, roughly bounded by Pico Boulevard, Motor Avenue, National Boulevard and the Beverlywood edge. It is inside the City of Los Angeles. Boundaries here are defined by street, not by listing description, so we confirm the parcel rather than the label.

What kind of homes are in Cheviot Hills?

Predominantly pre-war single-family — Spanish Colonial Revival, Tudor and Traditional houses on curving, hilly streets, with a layer of later remodels and some substantial rebuilds. Lots are generally larger and less regular than the Rancho Park flats below, and grade is a real design factor rather than a footnote.

Is Cheviot Hills a good place to live?

It suits buyers who want a quiet, low-density single-family street inside the city, walking or short-driving distance to Century City and Westwood, with a neighborhood park and golf course adjacent. What it does not offer is walkable retail density or much attached housing — for that, Palms and Sawtelle sit immediately south.

What schools serve Cheviot Hills?

The neighborhood falls inside Los Angeles Unified School District boundaries, with Overland Avenue Elementary the school most associated with the area. Assignments are made by address and boundaries change, so confirm the assigned school with LAUSD for the specific property before you treat school access as settled.

How does Cheviot Hills compare with Beverlywood?

They adjoin, and the housing looks similar from the street, but the governance differs. Much of Beverlywood sits under a homeowners association with architectural and maintenance rules that affect what you can build and how the exterior must be kept. Cheviot Hills is generally not subject to that regime. If a listing straddles the line, the association question comes before the offer.

What about ADUs and additions in Cheviot Hills?

California ADU law applies and the larger lots help, but grade is the constraint that decides most Cheviot Hills projects. Slope affects excavation, drainage, retaining, access for construction and the buildable envelope. Michael trained as an architect, and on a hillside parcel that read is the difference between a project that pencils and one that quietly does not.

Is there rent control in Cheviot Hills?

The neighborhood is overwhelmingly single-family, so the Los Angeles Rent Stabilization Ordinance is rarely the issue it is in Palms or Sawtelle. Where a property does include a rental unit — a converted structure, a duplex, or an ADU being rented — the applicable rules depend on the building and its certificate of occupancy date. This is general information, not legal advice; confirm with the Los Angeles Housing Department or your attorney.

What is nearby?

Cheviot Hills Recreation Center is the neighborhood green, Rancho Park Golf Course and the Hillcrest Country Club sit along the southern and western edges, and Century City is minutes north with offices, the mall and the medical center. The Metro E Line station at Westwood and Pico puts rail within reach of the neighborhood's southern edge.
