02

Our first step: understand the full property

Before determining the final pricing and marketing strategy, we would first want to fully understand what is being sold.

What we would review

  • Current rent roll
  • Who the tenants are and how long they have occupied the units
  • Current lease terms
  • Rent-control / RSO status
  • Actual operating expenses
  • Legal and permitted unit count
  • Any unpermitted structures, conversions, or additions
  • Status of the partially completed rear studio / ADU
  • Any outstanding code, permit, or property-condition issues
  • Recent improvements or major system upgrades
The prior marketing appears to contain inconsistencies regarding unit configuration and ADU status. Resolving those questions upfront gives buyers a much clearer picture and reduces uncertainty during escrow.

05

Private investor strategy

We do not wait for the right buyer to find this property. We go find him.

AMRE sells small multifamily by working a named list of investors directly — not by posting a listing and hoping the market returns to it. That is the part of this assignment we would do differently, and it is the part most likely to sell 1711–1715 Toberman.

Because the property has already had significant public MLS exposure, we would not necessarily recommend immediately putting it back on the full MLS. Our first approach could be to bring the opportunity directly to our private investor network.

Who we would go to first

Multifamily investors 1031 exchange buyers Value-add operators Local landlords Developers Cash-flow investors Compass investor relationships Outside brokers with active multifamily buyers

This lets us test pricing and buyer appetite privately, without immediately adding another public listing cycle to the property's history. We can speak directly with qualified investors, explain the opportunity, answer questions, and gather meaningful feedback.

Path A

A buyer emerges privately

If the right buyer is found through our network, there may be no reason to bring the property back to the full market at all.

Path B

The feedback sharpens the offer

If the private campaign does not produce a buyer, real investor feedback refines pricing and positioning before any relaunch.

Then

A stronger public return

Any broader launch happens with cleaner documentation, a better financial story, and a price the market has already spoken to.

06

Professional investor package

Rather than relying primarily on an MLS listing, we would create a clear investor package that allows a buyer to quickly understand and underwrite the property.

What the package would include

  • Unit-by-unit rent roll
  • Current versus market rents
  • Lease and tenant information
  • Operating expenses
  • NOI and cap-rate analysis
  • Rent-control information
  • Parcel and zoning information
  • Legal / permitted unit status
  • ADU status
  • Property photos, site and building information
  • Recent improvements
  • Potential income and value-add opportunities

It answers the investor's questions immediately

What am I buying?
What does it earn today?
What is the upside?
What are the risks?

And above all: what makes this opportunity compelling compared with other Los Angeles multifamily properties?

07

Better buyer access

The previous listing indicated showings with an accepted offer. If practical given the tenants, we would look for a way to provide serious, qualified investors with better access before requiring them to write an offer.

Asking a buyer to commit to a 12-unit property without enough visibility into the physical condition creates unnecessary friction and uncertainty. We would balance tenant privacy with giving qualified buyers enough information to become comfortable with the asset.

08

What we would do differently

Rather than simply placing Toberman back on the MLS and waiting for inquiries, we would:

Understand the property first

Rent roll, tenants, expenses, permits, ADU status — verified before pricing.

Resolve questions before buyers raise them

Every inconsistency answered in the package, not in escrow.

Package the financial story professionally

Underwriting-grade material for a small-balance asset.

Create a clear value proposition

One sentence an investor can repeat to a partner or a lender.

Directly approach qualified investors

Named outreach, not passive syndication.

Test the opportunity privately first

Gather real feedback before considering another full MLS launch.

09

Summary

The opportunity is not simply to put 1711–1715 Toberman back on the market. The opportunity is to reset the story.

We would first understand the full scope of the property, clarify the tenant, permit, unit, and ADU issues, package it as a serious investment opportunity, and then proactively bring it to qualified investors.

If the right buyer can be found through our private network, we may be able to sell the property without another full public relaunch. If broader exposure becomes necessary, we would then return to the market with a much stronger financial story, cleaner documentation, better positioning, and a pricing strategy informed by actual investor feedback.
